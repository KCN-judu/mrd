## Selected theme

An unfinished map hidden inside geometry.

The audience discovers the work as a sequence of chambers. Paper gives way to
an exact polygon. Chords become paths, crossings become a hidden network,
ordered coordinates reveal compact blocks, and a minimum cut supplies the key.
The final dissection is calm and exact. A last corridor remains unlit because
the implementation has not yet reached the full formal-boundary and
almost-linear-flow frontier.

## Emotional curve

Curiosity -> historical gravity -> clear discovery -> controlled complexity ->
quiet revelation -> evidence and trust -> calm resolution with one open question.

## Visual motifs

- Map: faint routes, discovered paths, rooms, and chambers.
- Page edge: becomes polygon boundary and later a timeline.
- Gold clue: used only for a newly established invariant or selected cut.
- Cool cyan: current computational state and active exact relation.
- Negative space: lets difficult structures become legible.

## Material and camera

Archive material is asymmetrical, shallow-focus, and lit like a research table,
with self-generated paper rather than reproduced page scans. Mathematical space
settles into frontal, sharp diagrams. Spatial moves occur only when they clarify
layers or topology. The camera does not float continuously.

## Sound motif

Paper friction and pencil contact begin the trail. A four-note motif appears as
isolated tones, aligns with the four coordinate comparisons, thins during flow,
and resolves after the final rectangle. Silence marks the minimum cut.

## Why the viewer continues

Each scene answers the previous scene's question while exposing a more precise
one: where are the decisive cuts, which choices conflict, how can a dense graph
stay compact, how does the cut recover geometry, and what remains unfinished?

## Reference translation

No named director or film is used as an identity template. The brief already
specifies documentary macro focus, motivated transitions, restrained dolly
movement, and spatial mathematical staging. These are translated into shot
functions rather than copied compositions, palettes, or signature techniques.
